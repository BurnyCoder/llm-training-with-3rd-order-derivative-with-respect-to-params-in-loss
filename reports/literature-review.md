# Literature review

**Question.** Does explicitly discouraging variation of the parameter Hessian improve out-of-sample next-token predictions? The search covered parameter-Hessian penalties, high-order derivative regularization, tensor norm estimation, automatic differentiation, compact LM benchmarks and public implementations. Input derivatives, optimizer approximations, and parameter-loss derivatives answer different questions.

### Third derivatives with respect to language-model weights

Yang et al. use a third weight derivative of minibatch training loss in a Taylor expansion of the Adam update. Their FUT++ method takes finite differences of approximate Hessians at successive reference checkpoints. They compare predictions for reordered batches against actual retraining of a 636-million-parameter LLaMA-style model on WikiText-103. Those measurements use the reference training trajectory. Our regularizer samples directions independently in weight space to estimate a squared norm over all mixed third partials. [Yang et al., §5.1 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22042#page=6) · [Appendix A.1, equations 14–15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22042#page=15)

McGee et al. derive a cubic model using a directional slope and two symmetric function evaluations along an optimizer step. In their language-model fine-tuning runs, GRPO supplies the slope and a separate reward-minus-KL score supplies the function evaluations. The fitted cubic combines these quantities to select a step size. Interpreting its coefficient as one objective's directional third derivative requires the slope and function values to come from that same objective. [McGee, Bergou and Dutta, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02190) · [Implementation, Appendix C.1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02190#page=28)
